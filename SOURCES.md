**Dimensions.** Nigiri are about 6–7 cm long and 2.5–3 cm wide, on rice about 2 cm high, with a topping 5–7 mm thick. A
medium roll piece is about 4–5 cm across and 2.5–3 cm tall. A serving board for a few pieces is about 20–25 cm long.

**Facts behind the one-liners** (common culinary knowledge, cross-checked against Wikipedia "Sushi", "Geta (sushi)",
"Makizushi", "California roll", "Wasabi", "Ikura" and "Salmon as food"):
- The wooden board sushi is served on is named after, and shaped like, a wooden sandal on two feet.
- Sushi rice is short-grain rice seasoned with rice vinegar, sugar and salt, and nigiri rice is served at about body warmth.
- In nigiri, a dab of wasabi usually goes between the rice and the fish. Its heat comes from compounds that rise into the nose and fade quickly.
- A roll is made by spreading rice on a sheet of nori, laying fillings along it, rolling it with a bamboo mat and cutting it into pieces.
- Avocado entered sushi in 1960s–70s Los Angeles (the California roll), in place of fatty tuna.
- Lean red tuna is the meat along the back and sides. Edo-period sushi stalls marinated tuna in soy sauce to keep it.
- Raw salmon sushi became popular in Japan from the late 1980s, after a Norwegian campaign to sell farmed salmon there.
- Salmon roe is cured in soy sauce or salt.
